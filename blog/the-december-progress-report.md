# The December Progress Report: How to Show Your Supervisor Real Dissertation Progress

![The December Progress Report: How to Show Your Supervisor Real Dissertation Progress](https://i.ibb.co/fdstN1c7/December-Dissertation-Progress-Report.jpg)

If your supervisor requests an update on the dissertation when you have no chapters finished, you may feel unprepared and seek [dissertation help](https://www.theacademicpapers.co.uk/dissertation-writing-services/). But research progress isn't just written work. Refining your research question, exploring relevant literature and reconsidering initial ideas all contribute to your dissertation.

Knowing how to write a **progress report for dissertation** can help you present your work with greater confidence. Important decisions can be highlighted, and challenges and tasks identified for further action. A realistic dissertation timeline also helps you plan and meet key deadlines. This guide is designed to help you show progress, reflect on your research experience and plan for your next steps effectively.

## What Is A Dissertation Progress Report For?

A dissertation progress report is a document used to discuss your research activities, completed work and remaining objectives with your supervisor. It recognises areas of difficulty that may be preventing you from conducting research, including limited resources, research issues, changing research directions, and more. This provides an opportunity to get feedback and address problems before submission. Formal progression assessments are also required for research students at some universities.

The University of Huddersfield, for instance, asks doctoral students to present a written progression report for a viva voce. Its guidance suggests discussing completed research, the project objectives, problems encountered, and proposed further work. Students must also address ethical approval and relevant training requirements. However, the same formalities are not necessarily followed with undergraduate or taught master's dissertations. Please read and follow your university’s reporting guidelines.

## Why Does Timing Matter This Term?

A dissertation progress report prepared near the end of term helps students review completed work, identify unfinished tasks and adjust their dissertation timeline before the next assessment period. Planning matters because deadlines, examinations, placements, and holiday closures can affect research time. Students are responsible for confirming supervisors' feedback schedules. Early identification of commitments enables realistic goals to be established and great work to be prioritised.

As shown in the UCL 2026-27 calendar, remember dates on the college site: the first term ends on 18 December 2026, and 9 November is listed as a College Reading Week date. These dates are not a UK-wide timetable. According to the 2026 Student Academic Experience Survey, commissioned by Advance HE and HEPI, 65% of full-time undergraduates work during the term. Employed students averaged 44.2 combined study and work hours per week. This emphasises the importance of working out reports in advance and referring to the university's academic calendar.

## How Do You Write A Progress Summary When Progress Feels Thin?

A Dissertation Progress Report should illustrate actual research activities, decisions and developments, not merely time spent working. Students can demonstrate progress without having finished any written work in specific examples.

Target the following areas:

- **Research question refinement:** Describe how the topic of research has evolved. *“My research question evolved from patient satisfaction to patient experiences of post-operative pain management, so that I could provide a more focused research direction.”*

- **Literature searches:** Recognise databases consulted and literature reviewed. “*I conducted a literature search in CINAHL and MEDLINE, read 120 abstracts and reviewed 25 relevant full texts for inclusion.”*

- **Methodological decisions:** Describe the research strategy and its rationale. *"I chose semi-structured interviews rather than questionnaires because the study explores participants' experiences."*

- **Ethical approval:** Report the current application status. *"I submitted my ethics application on 20 October and am awaiting approval."*

- **Research challenges:** Describe challenges and measures undertaken. *"Limited access to relevant studies led me to request additional articles through the university library."*

Use these details to develop a realistic dissertation action plan and to inform your next supervisor meeting.

## What Should The Dated Action Plan Look Like?

The dissertation progress report should be translated into a realistic completion date and a workable schedule of tasks that need to be completed. Students should allow for enough time for supervisor feedback, revisions and unanticipated delays, working backwards from their final submission date.

The plan can be organised as follows:

1.  When is the submission deadline?

2.  Identify examination placements and University Holidays.

3.  Break down big research steps into small weekly steps.

4.  Give the supervisor ample opportunity to comment and proofread.

For example:

- **Week of 12 November:** Screen 120 remaining abstracts and select 25 relevant studies.

- **Week of 21 November:** Complete 1,500 words of the literature review.

- **Week of 30 November:** Present the methodology draft to the supervisor – length 2000 words.

- **Week of 14 December:** Incorporate feedback and finalise the research methodology.

Each task should have measurable outcomes. Students should also revise their dissertation schedule if there are changes in research needs or conditions.

The University of Sheffield also recommends breaking dissertation work into manageable stages, with estimated durations and target completion dates to help students monitor progress and meet their final submission deadlines.

## How Do You Write A Reflection That Is Not An Apology?

A reflection in a dissertation progress report should demonstrate learning, self-awareness, and improvement rather than simply apologising for unfinished work. Students should be honest about the challenges they faced and how they have impacted their research.

Take these three questions into account:

- What happened? Discuss a research problem or a decision that was not successful.

- What did you learn? Discuss the impact of the experience on the research process.

- What will you change? Determine action steps for future research activities.

For example*: "I underestimated how much academic literature it would take to read, so I found it helpful to read abstracts and then the full texts to focus on relevant studies and make modifications to my research plan."*

This illustrates how problems can lead to improved decision-making and more realistic planning.

## What Three Sentences Make A Supervisor Meeting Productive?

A dissertation supervisor meeting is for discussing research progress, clarifying uncertainties, and agreeing on next steps. Students are encouraged to come prepared with specific questions, not just general updates. The following 3 statements can be used to structure the discussion:

1.  Clarifying research decisions*: "I have narrowed my research question, but I would like your input about whether it is the right amount of research for me to do.*” This will help validate the research line.

2.  Priorities: *"I'm going to finish my literature review before 30 November. Is there anything that I should focus on?*” This sets a realistic expectation for the next stepping stone.

3.  Brainstorming possible issues*: "Is there anything I need to adjust with my methodology or research timeline before I can continue?"* This enables problems to be solved before they escalate.

Birkbeck, University of London, suggests submitting draft work at least 5 working days before meetings. It also recommends that students take notes during meetings and capture action items. Students should take this feedback on board and include it in their dissertation action plan, including who is responsible and when it will be done.

## Suggested 750-word Dissertation Progress Report Breakdown

- Introduction and research overview: 75 words

- Research aims and objectives: 75 words

- Work completed so far: 175 words

- Literature review progress: 100 words

- Methodology and research developments: 100 words

- Challenges and difficulties: 75 words

- Remaining tasks and dissertation action plan: 100 words

- Conclusion and next steps: 50 words

*Note:* This is a suggested structure. Students should follow their university's specific reporting requirements and word limits. Reviewing a dissertation progress report example can help students understand how to present completed tasks, ongoing work and any challenges clearly.

## Common Mistakes In Dissertation Progress Reports

- Describing effort ("I worked hard") instead of identifying completed research activities.

- Failure to include methodological decisions or changes in research goals.

- Hiding difficulties that could affect your dissertation timeline.

- Vaguely listing future activities, e.g., "When the literature review is completed, I will…", but never stating dates.

- Failure to adhere to university-specific word limits, reporting formats and submission instructions.

A clear, honest and structured dissertation progress report is more useful than an overly positive report that conceals unfinished work.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Long Should A Dissertation Progress Report Be?

This length will vary depending on the university, programme and reporting stage. Some formats require a 750-word summary of progress, personal reflection and dated action plan. Always check the module handbook or supervisors' instructions before drafting.

### What If I Am Behind Schedule?

Be honest about unfinished tasks and explain the reasons for delays. Add an updated dissertation action plan including realistic deadlines and specific tasks. Communicate with your supervisor about key problems early in the process, rather than waiting until the paper is close to being submitted.

### Should I Include A Gantt Chart?

Gantt chart is used to visualise your dissertation timeline, the work content, its deadlines and activities that can overlap. However, check whether your university permits this format. You can also effectively communicate upcoming milestones with a simple task list with dates.

### Can A Progress Report Support An Extension Request?

Yes. It can document research challenges, missed deadlines and strategies for mitigating delays. Progress Reports do not necessarily equate to an extension. Follow the extension or mitigating circumstances procedure that your university may have and present any evidence that is required.

### What Counts As Progress At The Proposal Stage?

Progress may range from revising your research question, creating a search strategy, screening literature, choosing a methodology, writing an ethics application and setting a reasonable research timeline. Completed dissertation chapters are not required to show meaningful progress.

## Final Thoughts

A dissertation progress report should reflect meaningful research progress and not pages completed. Think about what you have done, what difficulties you have faced and what realistic steps you have taken. Reflect on your supervisor's feedback to adjust your strategy and troubleshoot promptly. Consistent planning and honest reporting will keep you on track and progressing with confidence toward your eventual graduation with your dissertation.
